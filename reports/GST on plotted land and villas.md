# Plot sales escape GST, villa builds do not

**Bottom line:** a developed residential plot sold as a plot is a **sale of land under Schedule III para 5, outside GST**. That holds even when the developer has laid roads, drainage, water and power lines first. CBIC Circular 177/09/2022 says so, and so does the Gujarat High Court in Munjaal Manishbhai Bhatt (2022). The earlier Gujarat, Karnataka and Madhya Pradesh rulings that taxed developed plots at 18% have been overtaken. Tax comes back in through two doors. The first is **separately itemised service lines**: development charges, club, maintenance, legal and transfer fees, mostly at 18%. The second is **villa construction**. When the same developer sells the plot and then builds the villa in its own project, rulings treat the two agreements as one residential real-estate project (RREP) supply. That supply is taxed at **5% (or 1% affordable) with no ITC, after deducting one-third for land**, not 18% with ITC. A plot resale between buyers is outside GST. The developer's transfer or NOC fee is taxable at 18%. From 1 April 2026, income-tax TDS on property of ₹50 lakh or more sits in **s.393(1) of the Income-tax Act 2025** (formerly s.194-IA). GST 2.0 (22 September 2025) made works contracts a flat 18% and, as reported, left the RREP rates unchanged. Several points rest only on secondary sources or single Advance Ruling (AAR) orders. These are marked **[secondary]** below, and an ERP should keep every line's treatment configurable.

## The plot price is land, provided development is not sold to the buyer

The statutory frame is simple. Schedule III para 5 treats "sale of land" as neither goods nor services. Schedule II para 5(b) taxes construction of a complex intended for sale only for buildings and civil structures before the completion certificate. **[secondary]** The statute text was not re-fetched; this is standard law.

The fight was over whether developing land turns it into a "construction service". Between 2020 and 2021 Gujarat said yes: the AAAR in **Shree Dipesh Anilkumar Naik (GUJ/GAAAR/APPEAL/2021/35)** taxed a developed plot at 18% under para 5(b) ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/aaar-confirms-18-per-cent-gst-sale-developed-plots.html)). Shree Construction and Sukhjit Commerce LLP followed the same line, as did Karnataka's Maarq Spaces. Maarq Spaces, however, concerned a joint-development service to a landowner, not a sale to a buyer ([SBS & Co](https://sbsandco.com/gst-implications-on-developemnt-of-plots-dec-2020/)).

Two things reversed this in 2022. In **Munjaal Manishbhai Bhatt v. UoI (Gujarat HC, 6 May 2022)** the court quashed those rulings. It held that land developed first and sold under a later agreement involves no service to the buyer. Only development done under a contract with the buyer is a service ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gujarat-hc-delivers-reliefs-real-estate-industry-india.html); [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/48062053/)). Then **CBIC Circular 177/09/2022 (3 August 2022), para 14**, said that land after levelling and laying drainage, water and electricity lines is still land and attracts no GST. Development services the developer *receives* from contractors stay taxable ([CBIC](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/pdf/cir-177-08-2022-cgst.pdf)).

Later rulings fall in line. The **Telangana AAR in Vaishnaoi Infratech (30 September 2023)** held plot sales non-taxable but taxed separate levelling and drainage services at 18% as works contract ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-sale-developed-plots-plot-development-service.html)). The **Karnataka AAAR in Rabia Khanum (2023)** went further. Where development is mandated by the planning authority, even advances received *before* development is complete are consideration for land ([TaxTMI](https://www.taxtmi.com/article/detailed?id=11333)). Cases from 2025 and 2026 widened Schedule III para 5 to cover assignment of leasehold land rights, and the Supreme Court dismissed the Revenue's appeal on 21 July 2026 ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/sales-taxes-vat-gst/1821078/no-gst-on-assignment-of-leasehold-rights-in-land-and-building-supreme-court-dismisses-departments-appeal)). **[secondary]** No CBIC circular after Circular 177 on plotted development was found, but the 2023–2026 circular index was not searched directly.

The **one-third land deduction** in Notification 11/2017-CT(R) para 2 is irrelevant to a bare plot, because the whole plot price is already outside GST. It matters for villas. Munjaal Bhatt held the *mandatory* deemed one-third ultra vires s.15 and Article 14 where the actual land value can be ascertained. Commentators read that as making the deduction optional ([Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/deduction-of-one-third-of-total-consideration-towards-the-value-of-land-is-arbitrary-hc/); [RMPS](https://rmpsco.com/1-3rd-deduction-for-land-value-under-gst-is-optional-munjaal-manishbhai-bhatt-versus-union-of-india-gujarat-high-court/)). Critics note tension with the Supreme Court's reasoning in Mohit Minerals ([SBS & Co](https://sbsandco.com/the-hit-and-miss-of-gujarat-high-court-in-munjaal-manishbhai-bhatt-1-3rd-deduction-for-transfer-of-land/)). **Unknown:** whether any Supreme Court SLP against Munjaal Bhatt is pending or decided. One unsourced claim that the High Court "upheld the notification" probably confuses the case with its 2021 interim order, and has been excluded.

The remaining risk is a matter of timing and drafting. A plot booked *after* layout development is complete is low risk. A plot booked *before* development, where the agreement commits the developer to development works for the buyer, is exposed outside Gujarat and Karnataka precedent. Because the plot sale is a non-taxable outward supply, **ITC on development inputs must be reversed or blocked** under s.17(2)/(3).

## Extra charges are taxed line by line, except pure-agent costs

**Development or infrastructure charges** inside one composite plot price follow the land. Practitioners advise against itemising them, because the Gujarat rulings carved out and taxed the development part ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-sale-developed-plot-cbic-clarifications-vs-aar-gujarat-rulings.html)). If invoiced separately, treat them as an 18% service under SAC 9954. Government levies such as EDC/IDC passed through at actual cost may qualify as pure-agent exclusions under Rule 33. **No plot-specific ruling confirms this, and the Rule 33 text was not fetched.**

**Preferential location charges (facing, corner, park-facing)**: Circular 234/28/2024-GST (11 October 2024), which followed the 54th Council, treats PLC paid before the completion certificate as part of the composite construction supply, taxed at the construction rate ([irisgst](https://irisgst.com/gst-circular-no-234-234-28-2024-clarifications-regarding-applicability-of-gst-on-certain-services/)). The **Punjab & Haryana HC in DLF (13 May 2026)** upheld this for flats ([TaxO](https://taxo.online/latest-news/preferential-location-charges-plc-is-taxable-at-the-same-gst-rate-applicable-to-construction-services-and-cannot-be-treated-as-an-independent-supply-punjab-and-haryana-high-court/)). No authority addresses bare plots. The same composite logic says PLC on a plot follows the land and is outside GST, while PLC on a plot-plus-villa follows the villa rate. **This is an inference, not a ruling.**

**Club, maintenance, legal and connection charges**: the rulings split. The Maharashtra AAR (2021) taxed builders' "other charges" at 18% ([SAG Infotech](https://blog.saginfotech.com/18-percent-gst-other-charges-received-builder)). The Maharashtra AAAR (Puranik, 2022, flats) bundled water and electricity connection, meter deposit, development and legal charges into construction at the construction rate ([Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/other-charges-like-water-supply-connection-charges-electricity-meter-and-security-deposit-amounts-are-inextricably-linked-with-construction-of-residential-apartment-attracts-12-gst-aaar-directs-to/267285)). With plots there is no taxable principal supply to bundle into. Arguing that the whole bundle is outside GST is untested, so the safer default is 18% on each service line.

Non-refundable maintenance deposits and corpus funds are taxable advances ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/corpus-fund-collected-housing-society-treated-taxable-supply-advance-future-services.html); [constrofacilitator](https://constrofacilitator.com/gst-applies-to-one-time-maintenance-deposit-as-it-is-non-returnable/)). Electricity recovered at actual cost as a pure agent is not taxable ([Taxscan, WB AAR](https://www.taxscan.in/no-gst-on-reimbursement-of-electricity-charges-when-collected-at-actual-cost-basis-aar/494762)). All of these rulings concern flats or RWAs, not plots.

## Villa construction on the buyer's plot: the same developer means 5% with no ITC

Building a villa is a composite supply of construction service under heading 9954. It is a works contract under s.2(119) and Schedule II para 6(a). The real question is the rate entry.

In **Varaha Land (Karnataka AAR, KAR ADRG 19/2026, 16 March 2026)**, separate land and villa-construction agreements were treated as one composite supply under **entry 3(ia), the RREP entry**, with the one-third land deduction. Outsourcing the build to a contractor did not shift liability ([TaxScan](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/gst-payable-on-villa-construction-even-if-work-is-outsourced-aar-1444934)). In **Prime Property Developers**, the Kerala AAR treated villas in the developer's project as RREP units at 7.5% (5% effective). It held that the order of the construction agreement and the sale deed did not matter ([LegalEra](https://www.legaleraonline.com/news/kerala-aar-developer-liable-for-75-gst-irrespective-of-construction-agreement-entered-before-or-after-execution-of-sale-deed-870891)). **[secondary]** The order number and date of the Kerala ruling were not obtained, and neither original order PDF was read.

The answer is that **the two-agreement structure does not unlock 18% with ITC**. When the land seller and the builder are the same promoter in its own project, rulings collapse the arrangement into RREP construction: **5% (non-affordable) or 1% (affordable), no ITC**. Entry 3(xii) at **18% with ITC** fits a genuine works contract on land the buyer acquired independently, or a build by an unrelated contractor. Entries 3(iv) and 3(vi) (government and PMAY-type schemes) do not apply to a private villa developer.

One conflict needs checking: TaxGuru's summary of Varaha reports 18% under 3(xii) ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/outsourcing-defence-villa-construction-taxable-service-gst.html)). That figure probably restates the applicant's rejected contention. **Check the original order before relying on it.** Varaha applied the standard one-third; whether the actual land value could have been used instead was not tested in that ruling.

## Resale between buyers stays outside GST, but the transfer fee does not

A plot resale from the original buyer to a new buyer is a sale of land and carries no GST, whoever the seller is ([Terra Insight](https://www.terra-insight.com/insights/completion-certificate-flat-sale-no-gst-india/)). No AAR on resale of a developed plot was found.

**Assignment of booking rights in a plot** before the sale deed is strongly supported as outside GST. This rests on the Supreme Court's 21 July 2026 dismissal in Gujarat Chamber of Commerce, which upheld the Gujarat HC (3 January 2025), and on the Bombay HC rulings of 2026 ([TaxO](https://taxo.online/latest-news/22-07-2026-no-gst-on-assignment-of-industrial-leasehold-rights-supreme-court-affirms-gujarat-high-courts-landmark-verdict/); [EY](https://www.ey.com/en_in/technical/alerts-hub/2026/01/bombay-high-court-rules-gst-not-leviable-on-assignment-of-leasehold-rights)). Those cases concern leasehold land, so applying them to plot bookings is a reasoned extension. **Booking rights in an under-construction villa** remain contested ([TaxTMI forum](https://www.taxtmi.com/forum/issue?id=119120)). Older AARs taxed assignments at 18% under SAC 999794 ([TaxGuru, AP AAR](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-applicable-transfer-long-term-leasehold-rights-aar-andhra-pradesh.html)).

The **developer's transfer or NOC fee** is consideration for agreeing to tolerate an act (Schedule II para 5(e)). Default it to **18%, SAC 999794** ([NJ Jain, Monalisa CHS](https://njjain.com/case-law-updates/case-of-monalisa-cooperative-housing-society-by-maharashtra-bench-of-the-appellate-authority-for-advance-rulings/)).

**Transfer of a partly built villa contract** has no direct authority. The construction supply simply continues to the new buyer at the same rate. The ERP can do one of two things:

- **Option A:** keep the past invoices and bill future milestones to the new buyer.
- **Option B:** issue s.34 credit notes to the original buyer and re-invoice the new buyer. This is time-barred after 30 November following the financial year, and from 1 October 2025 it needs a registered recipient to reverse ITC ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-provision-invoice-credit-notes-related-sale-construction-flat-shop.html)). An unregistered original buyer can claim a refund under Circular 188/20/2022.

**Income-tax TDS:** from 1 April 2026, s.194-IA became **s.393(1) Table Sl. 3(i)**. The rate is 1% of the higher of consideration and stamp-duty value where that is ₹50 lakh or more, deducted by the transferee on each instalment ([TDSMAN](https://blog.tdsman.com/2026/05/tds-on-purchase-of-immovable-property-section-3931-194ia/); [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/section-393-income-tax-act-2025-tds-rules-rates-compliance-guide-44450.html/)).

In a resale there are two deductee streams: the new buyer pays the original buyer, and the new buyer pays the developer. TDS must be tracked per deductee PAN. A non-resident original buyer needs s.195-type rates. Money the developer collects on the original buyer's behalf is a liability, not revenue, and carries no GST. **Not verified:** whether the ₹50 lakh threshold is tested on the premium or on the full property value, and which form replaces Form 26QB. GST TDS under s.51 does not apply to private buyers.

## Where the authorities conflict

| Issue | One side | Other side | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Developed plots | Gujarat AAR/AAAR 2020–21: taxable at 18% | Circular 177 and Munjaal Bhatt: outside GST | Earlier rulings quashed, but they show how auditors think |
| One-third land deduction | Munjaal Bhatt: optional | Notification as worded; Mohit Minerals reasoning | SLP status unknown |
| Varaha Land rate | TaxScan: 3(ia) at 5% | TaxGuru: 3(xii) at 18% | Check the original order |
| Other charges | Maharashtra AAR 2021: separate supplies at 18% | Maharashtra AAAR 2022: bundled into construction | Unresolved |

## Decisions a Chartered Accountant must make

The CA must decide:

- whether plot agreements are drafted so that development is complete, or merely incidental, rather than contracted for the buyer;
- whether to itemise development charges at all;
- whether PLC, club, legal and connection charges on bare plots are bundled with the land or taxed separately;
- whether same-developer villas go under 3(ia) at 5% with no ITC, or can defensibly be 3(xii) at 18% with ITC, and the ITC reversal that follows;
- whether to use the deemed one-third or the actual land value, given the state and the SLP risk;
- whether the project or a unit meets the affordable-housing criteria;
- how to compute the ITC reversal under s.17(2)/(3) for plot sales;
- whether the developer acts as pure agent for EDC, stamp duty and utility connections;
- whether to tax developer transfer fees;
- which option to use when a villa contract is transferred to a new buyer;
- how TDS is handled across the deductee streams;
- whether to seek an advance ruling in its own state.

The CA should also confirm the full text of Notification 15/2025-CT(R) for entries 3(iv)–(vi) and the SAC sub-codes. The CA should track the **57th GST Council (7 October 2026)**, where blocked-credit rationalisation after Safari Retreats is reportedly on the agenda ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/57th-gst-council-meeting-rescheduled-october-7-2026-legal-analysis.html)).

## Changes from 2024 to 2026

- **53rd Council (June 2024):** RERA collections made exempt (Circular 228/22/2024).
- **54th Council (September 2024):** PLC treated as composite (Circular 234/28/2024); RCM on commercial renting, reportedly Notification 09/2024-CT(R) **[unverified]**.
- **Supreme Court, Safari Retreats (October 2024):** a building could be a "plant" for ITC.
- **55th Council (December 2024):** the realty FSI RCM decision was deferred.
- **Finance Act 2025:** retrospectively replaced "plant or machinery" with "plant and machinery" in s.17(5)(d), undoing Safari Retreats. In force from 1 October 2025 by Notification 16/2025-CT ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-amendments-finance-act-2025-effective-oct-1-2025-notified.html)). The result is no ITC on a clubhouse.
- **56th Council and Notification 15/2025-CT(R) (from 22 September 2025):** works contracts at 18%; RREP 1%/5% reported unchanged **[secondary]** ([TaxO](https://taxo.online/latest-news/18-09-2025-cbic-issued-notifications-to-give-effect-to-the-recommendations-of-the-56th-council-meeting-in-respect-to-rate-and-exemption-on-services/)).
- **s.34 credit notes (from 1 October 2025):** a credit note cannot reduce output tax until a registered recipient reverses the ITC.
- **Income-tax Act 2025, s.393 (from 1 April 2026):** replaces s.194-IA for TDS on property.

## Conclusion

GST in plotted development now turns on contract design, not on physical development. Price the plot as one land amount, sold after development. Keep service lines few and clearly separated. The ERP should treat the villa as the developer's main taxable supply at 5% with no ITC. Three things could move the defaults:

- the original Varaha order;
- any Supreme Court ruling on Munjaal Bhatt;
- the 57th Council outcome.

So the ERP should store each line's status, rate, ITC flag and SAC as dated configuration, not hard-coded logic.
