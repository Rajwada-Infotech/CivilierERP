# GST and TDS on Investor Resale of Plots and Transfer of Villa Contracts (India, to Oct 2026)

## Q1. Resale of a plot by an individual investor (not in the course of business)

### Takeaway
A sale of land is outside GST under Schedule III para 5 ("sale of land") whoever the seller is. An investor who is a business (a dealer in plots) is still outside GST for the land itself. GST risk only comes from service elements bundled with it, such as development charges.

### Cited Findings
- Schedule III para 5 covers "sale of land and, subject to para 5 of Schedule II, sale of building". These are neither goods nor services, so a completed flat or building sold after the completion certificate is outside GST — [Terra Insight](https://www.terra-insight.com/insights/completion-certificate-flat-sale-no-gst-india/)
- Practitioners on TaxTMI say a one-off personal resale, not in the course of business, may fall outside GST. They also say characterisation and valuation can be disputed — [TaxTMI forum](https://www.taxtmi.com/forum/issue?id=119120)

### Inferences
- ERP: a plot resale (sale deed between investor and new buyer) should carry no GST line. The developer's own GST is limited to any taxable services it charges, such as the transfer fee in Q3.
- If the plot sale includes development or amenity charges, those parts are not "land". Whether they are taxable depends on how the developer's original contract was structured. This is covered by other research notes.

### Gaps
- I found no AAR ruling specifically on an individual investor reselling a developed plot. The Schedule III position is a statutory reading. I could not open the CBIC Schedule III text in this session.

## Q2. Assignment of allotment or booking rights before the sale deed

### Takeaway
For long-term leasehold rights in land, the case law is now settled in favour of the taxpayer. The Supreme Court dismissed Revenue's SLP on 21 Jul 2026, upholding Gujarat HC (Gujarat Chamber of Commerce, 3 Jan 2025): assignment is a transfer of immovable property, so no GST. Bombay HC followed this in 2026. For assignment of *booking rights in an under-construction flat or villa*, there is no direct ruling and the question is still contested.

### Cited Findings
- SC dismissed Union of India v. Gujarat Chamber of Commerce and Industry (Diary No. 33270-2025) on 21 Jul 2026. This upheld Gujarat HC's 3 Jan 2025 ruling that assignment of GIDC leasehold plot and building rights is a transfer of immovable property. The article says the ruling does not extend to booking rights. — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/sales-taxes-vat-gst/1821078/no-gst-on-assignment-of-leasehold-rights-in-land-and-building-supreme-court-dismisses-departments-appeal); [TaxO](https://taxo.online/latest-news/22-07-2026-no-gst-on-assignment-of-industrial-leasehold-rights-supreme-court-affirms-gujarat-high-courts-landmark-verdict/)
- Gujarat HC reasoning: under service tax, development rights were not taxable, and leasehold rights are a greater interest in land than development rights — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/sales-taxes-vat-gst/1821078/no-gst-on-assignment-of-leasehold-rights-in-land-and-building-supreme-court-dismisses-departments-appeal)
- Bombay HC held that assigning leasehold rights in an industrial plot with its building is not a supply of service (Jan 2026). The Nagpur Bench held the same in Hindustan Equipment Craft v. ACST, WP 1257/2026, 27 Feb 2026 — [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/01/22/bom-hc-no-gst-on-assignment-of-leasehold-rights/); [EY alert](https://www.ey.com/en_in/technical/alerts-hub/2026/01/bombay-high-court-rules-gst-not-leviable-on-assignment-of-leasehold-rights); [TaxTMI remand, Panacea Biotec, 21 Jan 2025](https://www.taxtmi.com/article/detailed?id=13439)
- Earlier AARs (e.g. Andhra Pradesh) held the opposite: assignment and transfer fees are taxable at 18% under SAC 999794. These AARs are effectively overtaken for leasehold land — [TaxGuru AAR AP](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-applicable-transfer-long-term-leasehold-rights-aar-andhra-pradesh.html); [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/18-gst-payable-on-transfer-of-leasehold-rights-aar/128502/)
- On reselling an under-construction flat before completion, experts disagree. One view: the transfer is taxable and no exemption applies. Another view: the builder stays liable on the construction service, the first buyer's transfer of rights may be outside GST, and a one-off non-business transfer is not a supply — [TaxTMI forum](https://www.taxtmi.com/forum/issue?id=119120)

### Inferences
- For a plot booking (land only), the SC-affirmed "benefit arising from land = immovable property" reasoning strongly supports treating assignment as outside GST.
- For a villa or construction booking, the risk is higher if the investor is in the business of trading bookings. If GST were levied, the likely classification is 18% (SAC 9997 or 9954-related). The ERP should flag the transfer as "assignor's own tax matter" and not compute GST on the premium.

### Gaps
- I did not find the Maharashtra AAR on assignment of flat booking rights that the brief mentions, so I cannot confirm it exists. I found no CBIC circular on booking-right assignment.

## Q3. Transfer or NOC fee charged by the developer

### Takeaway
A transfer fee or NOC fee that the developer charges for agreeing to substitute the buyer is consideration for the developer's own service. It is taxable at 18%. This is separate from whether the assignment itself is taxable.

### Cited Findings
- AARs treat a transfer fee as consideration for "agreeing to tolerate an act" (Schedule II para 5(e)), classified under SAC 999794 at 18% — [TaxGuru AAR AP](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-applicable-transfer-long-term-leasehold-rights-aar-andhra-pradesh.html)
- Maharashtra AAAR (Monalisa CHS) held that a transfer-linked payment by an outgoing member is liable to 18% GST — [NJ Jain](https://njjain.com/case-law-updates/case-of-monalisa-cooperative-housing-society-by-maharashtra-bench-of-the-appellate-authority-for-advance-rulings/)
- Maharashtra AAR applied 18% to a builder's "other charges" — [SAG Infotech](https://blog.saginfotech.com/18-percent-gst-other-charges-received-builder)

### Inferences
- Note that the leasehold judgments concern the assignor's consideration. Some commentators argue that a lessor's or developer's transfer fee is then also not taxable. Without a ruling on developer fees, the conservative ERP default is 18% under SAC 999794 (or 997212 when it is tied to the property), invoiced to the party paying it.

### Gaps
- I did not confirm whether GST 2.0 (Sept 2025) changed the residual 18% rate. I believe it did not, but I did not verify this.

## Q4. Transfer of a partly built villa construction contract

### Takeaway
The developer's construction supply continues. After novation it is supplied to the new buyer, at the same rate for the remaining instalments. Advances already invoiced to the first buyer are either left as they are, with the assignee reimbursing the assignor outside GST, or cancelled by credit note under Section 34 and re-invoiced to the new buyer. I found no specific authority on novation.

### Cited Findings
- On cancellation of an under-construction booking, the developer issues a Section 34 credit note. The deadline is 30 Nov after the end of the financial year, or the GSTR-9 filing date if earlier. An unregistered buyer can claim a refund within 2 years of the cancellation date under Circular 188/20/2022-GST — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-provision-invoice-credit-notes-related-sale-construction-flat-shop.html); [CBIC s.34](https://taxinformation.cbic.gov.in/content/html/tax_repository/gst/acts/2017_CGST_act/active/chapter7/section34_v1.00.html)
- From 1 Oct 2025, a credit note cannot reduce output tax unless a registered recipient has reversed the corresponding ITC — [Swipe](https://getswipe.in/blog/article/gst-on-credit-notes-under-section-34-of-the-cgst-act-conditions)
- The builder remains liable for GST on its construction services even when the booking is resold — [TaxTMI forum](https://www.taxtmi.com/forum/issue?id=119120)

### Inferences
- ERP options. (A) Keep the original invoices and receipts, record the assignee as the "customer from date X", and invoice future milestones to the assignee at the same rate. This is the simplest option, with no credit note. (B) Cancel and re-book with a credit note to the first buyer and fresh invoices to the assignee. This is cleaner for the customer's records but is time-limited by s.34.
- The rate depends on the project's character, which does not change: residential non-affordable 5% without ITC, affordable 1%, or a works contract at 18% if structured as a plot plus a separate construction contract. Status-based thresholds (e.g. the affordable price cap) may need re-checking if the assignee's price differs.

### Gaps
- I found no CBIC circular or AAR on novation of construction agreements.

## Q5. TDS: Income Tax s.194-IA (now s.393) and GST TDS

### Takeaway
From 1 Apr 2026, s.194-IA has been replaced by s.393(1) Table Sl. 3(i) of the Income-tax Act 2025. The tax is still 1% of the higher of consideration and stamp duty value, where that value is ₹50 lakh or more. The deduction is made by the transferee on each payment. GST TDS (s.51 CGST) applies only to government-type deductors, so it does not apply here.

### Cited Findings
- s.194-IA is replaced by s.393(1) [Table Sl. 3(i)] from 1 Apr 2026: 1% of the higher of consideration and SDV where ≥ ₹50 lakh — [TaxTMI notes](https://www.taxtmi.com/tmi_notes?id=1866); [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/section-393-income-tax-act-2025-tds-rules-rates-compliance-guide-44450.html/); [TDSMAN](https://blog.tdsman.com/2026/05/tds-on-purchase-of-immovable-property-section-3931-194ia/)
- The buyer deducts at the time of each payment or instalment. Under-construction instalments are covered once the agreed total is ≥ ₹50 lakh — [LegalSuvidha](https://legalsuvidha.com/blog/tds-on-property-purchase-194ia); [Income Tax India](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/tds-purchase-of-immovable-property)
- Some commentary doubts whether s.194-IA applies to a transfer of rights in a building (as opposed to the property itself) — [BCAJ](https://bcajonline.org/journal/transfer-of-immovable-property-tds-under-section-194-ia-analysis-and-issues/)

### Inferences
- In a resale there are two separate payee streams. (1) Assignee to investor (the premium or reimbursement): the assignee deducts 1% with the investor as deductee, if the amount is ≥ ₹50 lakh and the transaction is treated as immovable property. If the investor is a non-resident, s.195-equivalent rates apply instead. (2) Assignee to developer for remaining instalments: 1% with the developer as deductee. The ERP should not let the developer "collect on behalf of the seller" without tracking TDS separately for each deductee PAN. Any pass-through collection should be recorded as a liability to the investor, not as revenue, and should carry no GST.
- I found no source linking GST TDS to these transactions.

### Gaps
- I did not verify whether the ₹50 lakh threshold is tested on the assignment premium or on the whole property value in an assignment. I did not verify the Form 26QB replacement form under the 2025 Act ("Form 141" is mentioned by some blogs).
