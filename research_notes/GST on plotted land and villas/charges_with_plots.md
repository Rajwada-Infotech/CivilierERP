# GST on extra charges collected with residential plot sales (India, to Oct 2026)

## Development / infrastructure charges (EDC/IDC) collected from plot buyers

### Takeaway
CBIC says the sale of developed land, including levelling and drainage, water and electricity lines, is still a sale of land (Schedule III, item 5), so no GST is due. Gujarat AAR/AAAR rulings have instead taxed the development part at 18% as construction service. The risk goes up sharply when development charges are invoiced as a separate line.

### Cited Findings
- Circular 177/09/2022-TRU (3 Aug 2022), para 14: land may be sold as it is or after development such as levelling or laying drainage, water and electricity lines. A sale of such developed land is still a sale of land under Sch. III Sr. 5 and attracts no GST. Development services the developer receives (for example, laying drainage) are taxable at the applicable rate. — [CBIC circular](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/pdf/cir-177-08-2022-cgst.pdf); [taxguru summary](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-sale-developed-plot-cbic-clarifications-vs-aar-gujarat-rulings.html)
- Gujarat AAAR, Shree Dipesh Anilkumar Naik: a plot sold with water line, electricity line and other infrastructure is a sale of a developed plot. It was classed as construction service and taxed at 18%. — [taxguru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/aaar-confirms-18-per-cent-gst-sale-developed-plots.html)
- Sukhjit Commerce LLP (AAR-GUJ/GAAR/R/2021/37) and Shree Construction (AAAR-GUJ/GAAAR/APPEAL/2021/36) treated the infrastructure part as a taxable supply of service. These rulings bind only the applicant, while CBIC circulars bind the department. Practitioners advise against itemising development charges and recommend a single composite plot price. — [taxguru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-sale-developed-plot-cbic-clarifications-vs-aar-gujarat-rulings.html)
- Gujarat HC, Munjaal Manishbhai Bhatt v. UoI: the sale of land, developed or not, is not exigible to GST. Tax is limited to the construction activity. The court read down the mandatory 1/3rd land deduction in Notification 11/2017 para 2. That case was about a developed plot plus a bungalow. — [Khaitan](https://www.khaitanco.com/thought-leaderships/Gujarat-High-Court-holds-mandatory-deeming-fiction-of-1/3rd-deduction-for-value-of-land-in-under-construction-real-estate-transactions-under-GST-as-ultra-vires-and-unconstitutional,-%E2%80%98reads-down%E2%80%99-holding-it-to-be-optional); [NJ Jain](https://njjain.com/articles/landmark-judgement-on-taxability-of-land-by-gujarat-high-court/)

### Inferences
- ERP default: if development is part of the plot price, treat it as non-taxable (Schedule III, no SAC). Keep a configurable flag so a separately invoiced "development charge" can be taxed at 18% under SAC 9954 (construction) for risk-averse or Gujarat-based setups.
- EDC/IDC that is a government levy (for example, Haryana EDC) and passed through at actual cost may also qualify for pure-agent exclusion if the Rule 33 conditions are met (see the pure agent section). This is not confirmed by a ruling specific to plots.

### Gaps
- I did not find a ruling outside Gujarat on separately invoiced development charges for plots, and no 2025–26 circular that revisits Circular 177.
- I did not find a ruling specific to government EDC/IDC pass-through on plots.

## Preferential location charges (PLC) on plots

### Takeaway
For flats and buildings, PLC is part of the composite construction supply and taxed at the construction rate. This was clarified after the 54th GST Council meeting on 11 Oct 2024 under s.168(1) and upheld by the P&H HC in DLF, May 2026. For a pure plot sale there is no construction principal supply. The same composite logic therefore suggests PLC is part of the land consideration and outside GST, but no authority addresses plots directly.

### Cited Findings
- 54th GST Council: PLC paid with construction consideration before the completion certificate is part of the composite supply, with construction as the principal supply, so it gets the same rate. On ready (post-CC) property, no GST applies to PLC. — [taxguru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-preferential-location-charges-real-estate-54th-gst-council-meeting.html)
- A Government of India clarification dated 11.10.2024 under s.168(1) says PLC forms part of the composite supply. — [taxo](https://taxo.online/latest-news/preferential-location-charges-plc-is-taxable-at-the-same-gst-rate-applicable-to-construction-services-and-cannot-be-treated-as-an-independent-supply-punjab-and-haryana-high-court/)
- DLF Ltd v. Commissioner CGST (P&H HC, 13.05.2026): PLC is part of the composite construction supply and not an independent supply. The case concerned flats. — [taxo](https://taxo.online/latest-news/preferential-location-charges-plc-is-taxable-at-the-same-gst-rate-applicable-to-construction-services-and-cannot-be-treated-as-an-independent-supply-punjab-and-haryana-high-court/)
- The 54th Council clarification covers residential, commercial and industrial complexes. It does not expressly cover plots. — [taxguru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-preferential-location-charges-real-estate-54th-gst-council-meeting.html)

### Inferences
- Facing, corner, park-facing and wide-road premiums on a plot are a price attribute of the land itself, so they are part of the land consideration and should be non-taxable. Default the ERP to "follows the principal line" (land is exempt). For plot plus villa under construction, PLC follows the construction rate.

### Gaps
- I found no AAR, circular or court ruling on PLC for bare plots.

## Club membership, maintenance, corpus, legal, registration and stamp-duty reimbursement, electricity and water connection

### Takeaway
Authority is split. Maharashtra AAR (2021) taxed builder "other charges" at 18% as separate supplies. Maharashtra AAAR (2022, flats) held water and electricity connection, meter deposit, development and legal charges to be naturally bundled with construction at the construction rate. For plots there is no taxable principal supply to bundle into. The safer reading is that each service line is taxed on its own (18%), except genuine pure-agent reimbursements and refundable deposits.

### Cited Findings
- Maharashtra AAR (Aug 2021): "other charges" (meter installation, water connection, advance and club-house maintenance before the society is formed, legal costs) are not consideration for construction and are taxable at 18%. — [SAG Infotech](https://blog.saginfotech.com/18-percent-gst-other-charges-received-builder); [realtyquarter](https://realtyquarter.com/other-charges-obtained-by-the-builder-will-be-subject-to-18-gst-aar/)
- Maharashtra AAAR (2022, Puranik Builders, flats) reversed this. Water connection, electric meter, meter security deposit, development charges and legal fees are inextricably linked to construction, form a composite supply, and take the construction rate (12% at that time). — [taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/other-charges-like-water-supply-connection-charges-electricity-meter-and-security-deposit-amounts-are-inextricably-linked-with-construction-of-residential-apartment-attracts-12-gst-aaar-directs-to/267285)
- Corpus or sinking fund collected by a society or RWA is a non-refundable advance for future services, not a deposit under the s.2(31) proviso, and is taxable. The ₹7,500 per member per month exemption applies to regular maintenance subscriptions. — [taxguru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/corpus-fund-collected-housing-society-treated-taxable-supply-advance-future-services.html); [moneylife – Karnataka AAR](https://www.moneylife.in/article/homeowners-contribution-of-over-rs7500-per-month-to-corpus-fund-of-housing-society-is-taxable-karnataka-aar/63431.html)
- GST applies to a one-time maintenance deposit when it is non-returnable. — [constrofacilitator](https://constrofacilitator.com/gst-applies-to-one-time-maintenance-deposit-as-it-is-non-returnable/)
- Electricity charges recovered at actual cost: WB AAR held no GST is due where the recipient qualifies as a pure agent under Rule 33 CGST Rules. — [taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/no-gst-on-reimbursement-of-electricity-charges-when-collected-at-actual-cost-basis-aar/494762)
- Telangana AAAR (01.04.2025): electricity and water charges bundled with renting are taxable as part of the bundle. This concerned renting, not sale. — [taxo](https://taxo.online/01-04-2025-gst-applies-on-utilities-like-electricity-and-water-charges-when-bundled-with-renting-of-property-aaar-telangana/)

### Inferences (ERP default per line, for a plot-only sale)
| Charge | Suggested treatment | SAC (inferred) |
|---|---|---|
| Club membership / club development | Taxable 18% (separate service) | 9995 / 9996 |
| Advance maintenance (non-refundable) | Taxable 18% | 9972 (real-estate services on fee basis) |
| Refundable maintenance / security deposit | Not consideration until forfeited or applied | – |
| Corpus fund passed to the society | Advance, taxable 18% per AAR view; some argue it is a pass-through deposit | 9972 |
| Legal / documentation charges | Taxable 18% | 9982 / 9972 |
| Stamp duty and registration fees (paid in buyer's name, actual cost, separately shown) | Excluded from value as pure agent (Rule 33) | – |
| Electricity / water connection: DISCOM or board fees at actual cost | Pure agent, excluded | – |
| Electricity / water connection with builder markup or own work | Taxable 18% | 9954 / 9986 |
| PLC on plot | Follows land (non-taxable) | – |
| Development charges inside plot price | Follows land (non-taxable); flag 18% if itemised | 9954 |

- Plots: the s.2(30)/s.8 composite rule taxes a bundle at the principal supply's rate. When the principal supply is land, which is outside GST, the bundle would arguably be outside GST too. This is untested. The Gujarat rulings and Maharashtra AAR show the department tends to carve out and tax service elements, so keep each line configurable.
- The ERP should support per-line: taxable / exempt / non-GST (Sch. III) / pure-agent reimbursement / refundable deposit, plus SAC and rate.

### Gaps
- I found no ruling that decides club, maintenance or legal charges specifically for plots. All the cited rulings concern flats or RWAs.
- I did not find a primary source verifying the SAC codes in the table. The rates also need verifying against the post-Sept-2025 GST rate rationalisation, where 18% appears to be retained for these services and residential construction is at 5%/1% without ITC. That claim is my own and not sourced here.
- Rule 33 pure-agent conditions (contract authorises, actual amount, separately shown, recipient liable) are summarised from general knowledge. I did not fetch the rule text.
