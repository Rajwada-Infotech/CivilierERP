# GST on Villa Construction on Customer-Owned Plot (Two-Agreement Structure) — India, current to Oct 2026

## Is it a works contract / Schedule II 6(a) service?

### Takeaway
Yes, construction of a villa for a customer is a supply of service (composite supply, construction principal) under heading 9954. Whether it is taxed as a residual works contract (18% with ITC) or as RREP construction (1%/5% without ITC) depends on whether the developer is building "intended for sale" in its own project — AARs treat developer-sold plot + construction as RREP.

### Cited Findings
- Karnataka AAR (Varaha Land Pvt Ltd, KAR ADRG 19/2026, 16 Mar 2026): developer's construction of villas held a supply of service under heading 9954, composite supply with construction as principal supply; outsourcing to an independent contractor does not remove the developer's liability since it contracts with buyers and is responsible for delivery — [TaxScan](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/gst-payable-on-villa-construction-even-if-work-is-outsourced-aar-1444934); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/outsourcing-defence-villa-construction-taxable-service-gst.html)
- Works contract service generally falls in Sl. No. 3(xii) of Notification 11/2017-CT(R) at 18% — [Busy](https://busy.in/gst-rates/construction-services/); [Notification 11/2017-CT(R)](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/hindi/pdf/central-tax-rate/Notification11-CGST.pdf)

### Inferences
- Section 2(119) definition (transfer of property in goods in execution of a contract for building/construction of immovable property) is met by a villa build contract; Schedule II para 6(a) treats it as service. (Statutory text not re-fetched in this session.)

### Gaps
- Primary statutory text of s.2(119) / Sch II 6(a) not fetched; standard law, low risk.

## Which rate entry applies: 3(xii) 18% vs RREP 3(i)-(ie) 1%/5% vs 3(iv)/(vi)

### Takeaway
Where the developer itself sold the land and then builds the villa in its project, AARs (Karnataka 2026, Kerala) apply the RREP entry 3(ia) (5% effective without ITC, 1% if affordable) with the one-third land deduction — not 3(xii) 18%. Pure 3(xii) 18% with ITC fits a genuine third-party contractor building on land the customer acquired independently.

### Cited Findings
- Varaha Land (Karnataka AAR, 16.03.2026): activity "falls specifically under Heading 9954(ia)" (construction of residential units intended for sale before completion); tax at applicable rates after the standard one-third deduction for land — [TaxScan via search summary](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/gst-payable-on-villa-construction-even-if-work-is-outsourced-aar-1444934)
- CONFLICT: a TaxGuru summary of the same ruling (dated 3 Apr 2026 article) states 18% under 9954(xii) with ITC — this appears to restate the applicant's alternative contention (works contract at 9%+9%), which per TaxScan was rejected — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/outsourcing-defence-villa-construction-taxable-service-gst.html). Verify against the original order on the Karnataka CTD AAR portal before relying.
- Kerala AAR (Prime Property Developers): villas in the developer's project are "residential apartments" in an RREP; developer liable at 7.5% (5% effective after 1/3 land deduction) under entry 3(ia) as amended by Notification 03/2019-CT(R), irrespective of whether the construction agreement was signed before or after the land sale deed; supplementary work charges same rate — [LegalEra](https://www.legaleraonline.com/news/kerala-aar-developer-liable-for-75-gst-irrespective-of-construction-agreement-entered-before-or-after-execution-of-sale-deed-870891)

### Inferences
- Entries 3(iv)/3(vi) (12%, government/specified affordable housing/single residential unit schemes such as PMAY beneficiary-led construction) are irrelevant for a private villa developer.
- No ITC under RREP 3(ia); ITC available only if 3(xii) 18% applies.

### Gaps
- Original ruling PDFs (Varaha, Prime Property) not fetched; Prime Property ruling number/date not obtained.
- CBIC FAQ (May 2019) on RREP treatment of plot + construction not verified in this session.

## 2025 rate rationalisation (56th GST Council, Sept 2025)

### Takeaway
Notification 15/2025-CT(R) dated 17.09.2025, effective 22.09.2025, amended 11/2017-CT(R): several 12% construction entries (government earthwork works contracts >75% earthwork, offshore oil & gas works contracts, their sub-contracts) moved to 18% with ITC. Residual works contract 3(xii) stays 18%; RREP rates 1%/5% unchanged as far as found.

### Cited Findings
- Notification 15/2025-CT(R), 17.09.2025, effective 22.09.2025 (with corresponding IGST 9/2025-IT(R) / UTGST notifications); 12%→18% for predominantly-earthwork government works contracts, offshore oil & gas works contracts, sub-contracts thereof, etc. — [TaxO](https://taxo.online/latest-news/18-09-2025-cbic-issued-notifications-to-give-effect-to-the-recommendations-of-the-56th-council-meeting-in-respect-to-rate-and-exemption-on-services/); [CBIC 9/2025-IT(R) PDF](https://courier.cbic.gov.in/ECCS/advisory/2025/NOTIFICATION%20NO.%209_2025-INTEGRATED%20TAX%20(RATE)%20-1759486719.pdf)
- Post-22.09.2025, 18% is the general rate for works contract (SAC 9954) — [Busy](https://busy.in/gst-rates/construction-services/); [Casurana](https://casurana.com/blogs/gst-works-contract-construction-services-guide.html)
- Superseded: Government works contracts already moved 12%→18% earlier via Notification 03/2022-CT(R) dated 13.07.2022 (effective 18.07.2022) — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-rate-increase-government-projects-works-contracts.html)

### Gaps
- Exact post-22.09.2025 status of entries 3(iv)/(v)/(va)/(vi) (whether retained at 12% or moved) not confirmed from the notification text; read Notification 15/2025-CT(R) directly. No source found indicating any change to RREP entries 3(i)-(ie).

## Does splitting into land sale + construction agreement change the position?

### Takeaway
Not materially, per AARs: two agreements with the same developer are treated as one composite supply of RREP construction (5%/1% with 1/3 land deduction, no ITC). Land sale itself is outside GST (Sch III para 5), but the deemed 1/3 deduction applies on the total value; the split does not unlock 18% with ITC nor exclude construction from RREP.

### Cited Findings
- Varaha Land: separate agreements for undivided land share and villa construction treated as single composite supply; construction principal; 9954(ia) with 1/3 land deduction — [TaxScan](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/gst-payable-on-villa-construction-even-if-work-is-outsourced-aar-1444934)
- Kerala AAR Prime Property Developers: sequence of construction agreement vs sale deed irrelevant; 7.5% (effective 5%) — [LegalEra](https://www.legaleraonline.com/news/kerala-aar-developer-liable-for-75-gst-irrespective-of-construction-agreement-entered-before-or-after-execution-of-sale-deed-870891)
- Sale of developed plots without construction advances held not a supply (land) — [A2Z Taxcorp](https://a2ztaxcorp.com/sale-of-developed-plots-without-receiving-any-advance-from-their-customers-for-undertaking-development-activities-is-neither-a-supply-of-goods-nor-a-supply-of-service/)

### Inferences
- For ERP design: when a plot sale is followed by a construction agreement with the same developer in the same project, default to RREP rate (5% non-affordable / 1% affordable) on construction consideration, consistent with AARs; offer 18%-with-ITC only for third-party build-on-own-land contracts. AARs bind only applicant; take professional advice.
- Whether 1/3 deduction applies when land value is separately invoiced vs. deemed: Varaha applied the standard 1/3; not verified whether actual land value can be used.

### Gaps
- No Tamil Nadu or Telangana AAR found in this session on two-agreement villa schemes.
- Joint development/landowner share: Varaha involved a JDA revenue-share; detailed treatment not researched.
