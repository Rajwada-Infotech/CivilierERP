# GST on sale of developed residential plots (India, current to Oct 2026)

## Q1. What Schedule III para 5 and Schedule II para 5(b) say, and how they apply to a developed plot

### Takeaway
Schedule III para 5 of the CGST Act treats "sale of land" as neither goods nor services. Schedule II para 5(b), which makes construction of a complex intended for sale a service, applies only to buildings and civil structures sold before the completion certificate. The disputed question is whether laying roads, drainage and other utilities turns a plot into a "construction service". The current weight of authority says no: CBIC Circular 177/09/2022 and the Gujarat High Court in Munjaal Bhatt (2022) treat a developed plot sold as a plot as a sale of land.

### Cited Findings
- **Earlier AAR/AAAR position (superseded):** a series of 2020-21 Gujarat rulings held the sale of developed plots taxable as a construction service under Schedule II para 5(b). Example: Gujarat AAAR, Shree Dipesh Anilkumar Naik, Order No. GUJ/GAAAR/APPEAL/2021/35 dated 12-12-2021. The applicant was a landowner whose Jilla Panchayat required sewerage and drainage, water line, electricity line and land levelling for roads before plots could be sold. The AAAR held this a taxable supply at 18% under Sch. II para 5(b) — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/aaar-confirms-18-per-cent-gst-sale-developed-plots.html); [PwC alert, Jul 2020, Gujarat AAR orders](https://www.pwc.in/assets/pdfs/services/tax/indirect_news_alert/2020/pwc_news_flash_8_july_2020_gujarat_aar_issues_gst_orders_on_sale_of_developed_plots.pdf)
- **CBIC Circular No. 177/09/2022-GST dated 03-08-2022:**
  - Para 14.2: sale of land is covered by Sch. III and does not attract GST.
  - Para 14.3: land after levelling, laying of drainage lines, water lines, electricity lines and similar works is still "land", so its sale is covered by Sch. III para 5 and does not attract GST.
  - Para 14.4: services received for developing the land (for example, drainage work done by a contractor) attract GST at the applicable rate.
  — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-sale-developed-plot-cbic-clarifications-vs-aar-gujarat-rulings.html)
- **Gujarat HC, Munjaal Manishbhai Bhatt v. UoI, 06-05-2022** (Spl. Civil Appl. 1350 & 6840/2021, 5052/2022; 2022 (5) TMI 397; [2022] 138 taxmann.com 117) quashed the Gujarat AAR/AAAR developed-plot rulings, including Dipesh Anilkumar Naik. Its reasoning:
  - If land is already developed and the agreement with the buyer is made afterwards, no service is supplied to the buyer; it is a sale of land.
  - Only development undertaken under a contract with the buyer (at the buyer's behest) is a service.
  - The court distinguished Narne Construction because that case arose under consumer-protection law.
  — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gujarat-hc-delivers-reliefs-real-estate-industry-india.html); [TaxManagementIndia](https://www.taxmanagementindia.com/visitor/detail_article.asp?ArticleID=10396); [Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/48062053/)

### Inferences
- The ERP default should treat the plot price of a plot sold as a developed plot (where the developer develops its own land before or independently of the buyer's agreement) as a non-GST Sch. III supply.
- Separately contracted development or amenity charges collected from the buyer are at risk of being treated as taxable services.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain the verbatim statutory text from cbic-gst.gov.in in this session. The wording in the Takeaway is the well-known text, but it has not been re-verified here.

## Q2. Notification 11/2017-CT(R) para 2 (deemed one-third land deduction), Munjaal Bhatt, and any Supreme Court ruling

### Takeaway
For construction services that involve transfer of land or undivided share, Notification 11/2017-CT(R) para 2 deems land to be one-third of the total amount and taxes the remaining two-thirds. Munjaal Bhatt held that mandatory deeming ultra vires and arbitrary under Article 14 where the actual land value is ascertainable. The deduction applies to buildings and villas, not to bare developed plots. I could not confirm the status of any Supreme Court SLP.

### Cited Findings
- **The challenge:** Entry 3(if) read with para 2 of Notification 11/2017-CT(R) dated 28-06-2017 was challenged. The petitioner had a bungalow (land plus construction) agreement with separate considerations for land and construction. The builder charged 18% on the whole amount less a deemed one-third for land — [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/deduction-of-one-third-of-total-consideration-towards-the-value-of-land-is-arbitrary-hc/)
- **The holding:** the mandatory one-third deeming fiction is ultra vires Section 15 and the scheme of the CGST Act, and discriminatory and arbitrary (Article 14). A deeming fiction should apply only where the actual land value cannot be ascertained. Commentators read the result as making the one-third deduction optional, with the actual land value deductible where it is separately ascertainable — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/construction-services%E2%94%80-validity-deduction-1-3rd-total-consideration-value-land.html); [RMPS](https://rmpsco.com/1-3rd-deduction-for-land-value-under-gst-is-optional-munjaal-manishbhai-bhatt-versus-union-of-india-gujarat-high-court/); [AMLegals](https://amlegals.com/mandatory-deduction-of-1-3rd-of-land-value-is-ultra-vires-the-cgst-act/)
- **Critique:** commentators note tension with the Supreme Court's later decision in UoI v. Mohit Minerals, which supports valuation by notification — [TaxO](https://taxo.online/latest-research/1-3rd-deduction-of-land-is-optional-and-deduction-for-land-can-be-claimed-based-on-actual-value-of-land/); [SBS & Co](https://sbsandco.com/the-hit-and-miss-of-gujarat-high-court-in-munjaal-manishbhai-bhatt-1-3rd-deduction-for-transfer-of-land/)
- **Conflicting claim:** one search-result summary said the Gujarat HC "later dismissed the writ applications, upholding the notification". I found no primary source for this. It is likely a confusion with the HC's earlier 27-01-2021 interim order ([Indian Kanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/187939714/)). **Treat it as unverified.**

### Inferences
- For villas or bungalows sold before the completion certificate, the ERP should support two ways of valuing land:
  - the deemed one-third (the default under the notification); and
  - the actual land value, where it is separately stated (supported by Munjaal Bhatt; strongest in Gujarat, persuasive elsewhere).
- For pure plot sales, the one-third mechanism does not apply, because the plot sale is outside GST altogether.

### Gaps
- **Supreme Court SLP status:** I could not confirm whether the Union filed an SLP against Munjaal Bhatt, or whether one is pending, decided or dismissed. No reliable 2023-2026 source turned up. Check the SC case-status portal.

## Q3. AAR/AAAR rulings on developed plots: taxable vs. exempt

### Takeaway
Before the August 2022 circular, rulings in Gujarat, Karnataka and Madhya Pradesh mostly held developed-plot sales taxable at 18%. After the circular and Munjaal Bhatt, the rulings I found treat the plot sale as exempt land and tax only development services supplied separately (Telangana 2023, Karnataka AAAR 2023).

### Cited Findings

**Rulings treating the sale as taxable (mostly superseded):**
- **Gujarat AAAR, Shree Dipesh Anilkumar Naik:** GUJ/GAAAR/APPEAL/2021/35, 12-12-2021. 18%, construction service. Quashed by the HC in Munjaal Bhatt — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/aaar-confirms-18-per-cent-gst-sale-developed-plots.html); [TaxGuru on quashing](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gujarat-hc-delivers-reliefs-real-estate-industry-india.html)
- **Gujarat AAAR, Shree Construction:** GUJ/GAAAR/APPEAL/2021/36. The development component is a taxable service — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-sale-developed-plot-cbic-clarifications-vs-aar-gujarat-rulings.html)
- **Gujarat AAR, Sukhjit Commerce LLP:** GUJ/GAAR/R/2021/37. GST on the development component — same source.
- **Gujarat AAR, 2020 batch of orders** on developed plots, all held taxable — [PwC](https://www.pwc.in/assets/pdfs/services/tax/indirect_news_alert/2020/pwc_news_flash_8_july_2020_gujarat_aar_issues_gst_orders_on_sale_of_developed_plots.pdf)
- **Karnataka AAR, Maarq Spaces Pvt Ltd:** 2019 (11) TMI 994.
  - Facts: a joint development of land into a residential layout, with revenue shared 75% to the landowner and 25% to the developer, and the developer bearing development costs.
  - Holding: a supply of service to the landowners, taxable at 18% — [SBS & Co](https://sbsandco.com/gst-implications-on-developemnt-of-plots-dec-2020/)
  - Note: this is a joint-development service to the landowner, not a sale to a buyer.
- **Commentary:** the Karnataka, Gujarat and Madhya Pradesh AARs were unanimous, before 2022, that developing land and selling plots is a service — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-sale-developed-plots-plot-development-service.html); [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/sale-of-developed-plot-is-different-from-sale-of-land-and-covered-under-supply-of-construction-service-aaar/)

**Rulings treating the sale as exempt land:**
- **Telangana AAR, Vaishnaoi Infratech and Developers Pvt Ltd, 30-09-2023** (2023 taxguru.in 6757). The correct spelling is "Vaishnaoi", not "Vaishnavi".
  - Sale of developed plots: not taxable (Sch. III).
  - Development services (levelling, drainage): taxable at 18% as works contract.
  - TDR under the development agreement: taxable at 18% under RCM at the promoter's end.
  — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-sale-developed-plots-plot-development-service.html)
- **Karnataka AAR (08-09-2022), upheld by Karnataka AAAR, Ms. Rabia Khanum** (2023 (2) TMI 929).
  - Facts: three acres subdivided into residential plots, with roads, drainage and utilities mandated under the Town & Country Planning Act.
  - Holding: transfer of title is the dominant intention, and the mandated development is incidental. All consideration, including advances received before development is complete, is for sale of land under Sch. III entry 5 and not taxable. Voluntary or additional development services charged separately remain taxable.
  — [TaxTMI](https://www.taxtmi.com/article/detailed?id=11333); [A2Z Taxcorp](https://a2ztaxcorp.com/sale-of-small-plots-of-land-with-development-work-is-not-taxable-under-gst/); [SAG Infotech](https://blog.saginfotech.com/karnataka-aaar-supports-aar-case-gst-applicability-plot-sales)

### Inferences
- The post-2022 trend is consistent: the plot price is exempt, and only separately contracted development services are taxable.

### Gaps
- **Names I could not verify:** "Ultra Space Developers" (Maharashtra AAR), "Shri Shrinivasa", "Satyam Developers" and "Nirmal Developers". Searches returned nothing, so I cannot confirm these rulings exist and have left them out.
- **States not covered:** I found no Tamil Nadu or Andhra Pradesh AAR on plotted development.
- **Original order PDFs:** not retrieved. Order numbers come from secondary sources.

## Q4. Splitting development value from land, and the rate

### Takeaway
Under the circular, the HC and the post-2022 AARs, there is no requirement to split the price of a plot the developer itself sold after developing it: the whole amount is land. Where development is charged as a separate supply, or done for the landowner or buyer under contract, it is taxed at 18%. Rulings variously describe it as works contract, construction service or residuary service, typically under SAC 9954. No part of a separately charged development fee is exempt.

### Cited Findings
- **Telangana:** development services (levelling, drainage) are taxable as works contract at 9% + 9% — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-sale-developed-plots-plot-development-service.html)
- **Gujarat AAAR (superseded):** 18% on the whole developed-plot value as construction service — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/aaar-confirms-18-per-cent-gst-sale-developed-plots.html)
- **Karnataka (Maarq Spaces):** 18%. The concessional rates are confined to apartment and residential construction, not plots — [SBS & Co](https://sbsandco.com/gst-implications-on-developemnt-of-plots-dec-2020/)
- **Practitioner advice:** quote a single composite plot price rather than itemising development charges — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-sale-developed-plot-cbic-clarifications-vs-aar-gujarat-rulings.html)
- **Input tax credit:** CBIC para 14.4 confirms that development services the developer receives (from contractors) attract GST — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-sale-developed-plot-cbic-clarifications-vs-aar-gujarat-rulings.html)

### Inferences
- **ERP design:** keep the plot base price GST-free (Sch. III / non-GST). Model optional separately invoiced items (development or amenity charges, club, maintenance) as taxable lines at 18%.
- **ITC impact:** because plot sales are a non-taxable outward supply, ITC on development inputs must be reversed or blocked under Section 17(2)/(3).

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative determination of the exact SAC (9954 vs 9997) for separately charged plot development.

## Q5. CBIC, GST Council and court developments after 2023

### Takeaway
I found no newer CBIC circular or GST Council clarification on plotted development after Circular 177/09/2022. Later case law (2025) reinforces a broad reading of Sch. III para 5 for leasehold land. Safari Retreats (SC, 2024) concerns ITC on buildings and is not directly relevant.

### Cited Findings
- **Gujarat HC, GIDC leasehold (Jan 2025):** assignment or transfer of long-term leasehold rights in GIDC plots falls under Sch. III para 5 and is outside GST. The Supreme Court dismissed the revenue's SLPs — [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/no-gst-on-assignment-or-transfer-of-long-term-leasehold-rights-for-industrial-plots-supreme-court-dismisses-slp-against-gcci-read-order-1449163); [CAClubIndia](https://www.caclubindia.com/judiciary/no-gst-levy-on-assignment-of-leasehold-rights-of-industrial-plots-as-supreme-court-dismisses-revenue-s-slps-6048.asp)
- **Gujarat HC, 11-07-2025:** transfer of leasehold rights is akin to sale of land and excluded under Sch. III — [TaxO](https://taxo.online/latest-news/11-07-2025-transfer-of-leasehold-rights-is-not-a-taxable-supply-under-gst-as-it-is-akin-to-sale-of-land-which-is-excluded-under-schedule-iii-gujarat-high-court/)

## Q6. Timing: before vs. after completion certificate; agreement to sell vs. sale deed

### Takeaway
For plots, the operative test from Munjaal Bhatt is whether the development was done under a contract with the buyer. Development finished before the buyer signs makes the sale pure land. The Karnataka AAAR (Rabia Khanum) went further and treated advances received before development is complete as land consideration too, where the development is mandated by the planning authority. The completion-certificate cutoff in Sch. II para 5(b) and Sch. III para 5 matters for villas and buildings, not for bare plots.

### Cited Findings
- **Munjaal Bhatt (Gujarat HC):**
  - Already-developed land sold under a later agreement: no service.
  - Development undertaken under the buyer contract: may involve a service.
  — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gujarat-hc-delivers-reliefs-real-estate-industry-india.html)
- **Rabia Khanum (Karnataka AAAR):** title passes only after development is complete. Advances received before then are still consideration for land, not taxable — [TaxTMI](https://www.taxtmi.com/article/detailed?id=11333)

### Inferences
- **Low-risk case:** plots booked after layout development is complete.
- **Higher-risk case:** plots booked before development where the agreement commits the developer to development works for the buyer, outside Karnataka and Gujarat precedent.
- **ERP suggestion:** store the booking date against the layout's development-completion date and flag the gap.
- **Villas:** the completion-certificate date, together with the one-third or actual-land valuation from Q2, should drive taxability.

### Gaps
- I found no ruling that specifically turns on agreement-to-sell vs. sale-deed date for plots.
